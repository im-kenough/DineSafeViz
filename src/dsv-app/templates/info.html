DineSafeViz Info

DineSafeViz sorts and visualizes food safety data from Toronto Public Health, sourced from the City of Toronto's Open Data portal. This page explains the infraction categories and inspection outcomes that appear in that data.

DineSafe™ is Toronto Public Health's food safety program. It inspects every establishment that prepares or serves food. Each inspection ends in a Pass, Conditional Pass, or Closed notice.

Types of infractions

An infraction is a violation of the Ontario Food Premises Regulation (493/17). There are three categories: crucial, significant, and minor.

Crucial

A crucial infraction poses an immediate health hazard and directly involves food, such as contamination, unsafe temperatures, or no safe drinking water.

Deadline
It must be fixed on the spot. If it isn't, the inspector can order the premises closed.
Enforcement
Action is always taken.

Examples

  • No hot and cold running water where food is prepared or utensils are washed
  • Rodent or insect infestation with no effective pest control
  • Inadequate refrigeration
  • Sewage back-up
  • No safe drinking (potable) water
  • Contaminated or adulterated food

Significant

A significant infraction is a potential health hazard. It involves food indirectly, through how food is handled, prepared, stored, or served.

Deadline
24–48 hours, or legal action may follow.

Examples

  • Food contact surfaces or equipment need cleaning or repair
  • Refrigeration or dishwashing equipment needs repair
  • No accurate thermometers
  • Hand wash basin missing or lacking supplies
  • Garbage not stored in a sanitary way
  • Equipment and utensils not properly cleaned and sanitized
  • Washroom not kept clean or stocked

Minor

A minor infraction is a minimal health hazard.

Deadline
The next inspection.

Examples

  • Walls, floors, or other non-food surfaces need cleaning or repair (for example, cracked floor tiles or peeling paint away from food prep areas)
  • Inadequate ventilation or lighting
  • Staff not wearing hair restraints

Inspection findings

Every food establishment in Toronto gets one to three inspections a year. The number depends on the type of establishment, how food is prepared, and how much and what kind of food is served.

Pass

Issued when
The inspector finds no infractions, or only minor ones.

If the same minor infractions show up at the next inspection, the inspector may issue a ticket. Fines range from $55 to $465, depending on severity.

Conditional Pass

Issued when
The inspector finds one or more significant infractions.

The inspector follows up with up to two re-inspections:

  1. First re-inspection (within 24–48 hours): If the issues are fixed and nothing worse than a minor infraction is found, the establishment gets a Pass. If they aren't fixed, a ticket is issued and a second re-inspection is scheduled.
  2. Second re-inspection: If the issues still aren't fixed, the operator gets a court summons and may be referred to Toronto's Municipal Licensing and Standards Division.

Closed

Issued when
A crucial infraction isn't fixed on the spot. An establishment can only be closed when a health hazard is present.
  • An Order to Close is issued, and the hazard must be removed.
  • An inspector visits daily to check that the establishment stays closed. Breaking the order brings more charges.
  • The establishment can reopen with a Pass once every infraction in the order, plus any other significant or crucial ones, is fixed.
  • Repeat or new crucial infractions can mean a court summons and a referral to the Licensing Tribunal.
